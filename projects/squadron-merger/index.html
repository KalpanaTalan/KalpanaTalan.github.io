New to defence? 4 key terms
Squadron:
An operational unit of the air force, with its own people and equipment.
Air defence:
Equipment that protects the nation's airspace, which must always be ready.
IMMOLS:
The inventory system that tracks spare parts.
Handover:
The complete transfer of equipment, and responsibility for it, to a new owner.

Decisions that mattered

Six choices that kept the merger safe

Decision 01

Scope upfront

Listed all requirements and milestones first: a one-month deadline left no room to find scope late.

Decision 02

Map first

Mapped both squadrons' records and assets, so mismatches showed before anything moved.

Decision 03

Five workstreams

Operational equipment, maintenance assets, admin assets, inventory data migration, and personnel and procedures, all moving at once.

Decision 04

Migrate with a safety net

Cleaned and matched records first, and kept both sets until the new inventory was verified.

Decision 05

One reporting line

One common format, a single point of contact and regular updates to senior leadership.

Decision 06

Verify in layers

Checklists per asset category, joint verification, handover inspection and a senior leadership review.

The four questions, answered

What worked

Scoping upfront, mapping first, five parallel workstreams and layered verification: one month, zero data loss.

What didn't (the honest note)

There is no before-and-after metric here. Success was nothing lost and the deadline met, which the Squadron recognised and senior leadership appreciated.

What I'd improve

A digital milestone tracker to give everyone a live view of progress, and a merger playbook so the next merger starts from a proven plan.

How I'd measure success

Zero data loss, every asset verified at handover, and the merger complete within the deadline while operations carried on.
