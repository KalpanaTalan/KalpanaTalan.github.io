The problem and why it mattered

48 hours on the clock, and four people in three countries

Pet Care Companion is an app where users create pet profiles and keep their pet's care in one place, with AI turning care instructions into editable checklists. The product was one challenge. Doing it as a team across time zones was the other.

The five features
Pet profiles
Feeding and walking routines
Vaccination records
Grooming and vet appointments
AI-generated editable checklists
The pain point
A pet owner has routines, records and appointments to keep track of, and care instructions that need turning into something they can actually follow.
The hurdle
Delivering a complete product in 48 hours while four people worked from three different countries.
Constraints
48 hours
Time zones across three countries
A suggested toolset, with the front-end tool left to us
Built end to end
Why remote teams usually struggle here
With time-zone gaps and a hard deadline, work can stall between handoffs and people can drift apart on scope. This project was a test of whether we could prevent that.

How we worked together

Four habits that turned four people into one team

Habit 01

Ownership

Each person owned a part of the build, with a clear owner for every task.

Habit 02

One plan

The team playbook was the single source of truth, so everyone worked from the same plan.

Habit 03

Staying in touch

Overlap windows for live calls, plus short updates in the team chat.

Habit 04

Handoffs

A status note at the end of each person's day, so the next time zone could pick up.

My part

One of four, and a mix of everything

Me

Coordinated across time zones, built the backend and pet records, and worked on the pitch and demo. Keeping all five features in scope raised the pressure on a 48-hour plan, so clear ownership and written handoffs mattered even more.

What I would do differently

Settle the ground rules in the first hour

The lesson
Remote work holds up when everyone knows who owns what, what the plan is and how work passes from one time zone to the next.
Overlap hours
Agree them on day one, instead of working them out as we went.
A scope agreement
Write a short one upfront, so everyone is aligned on what is in scope from the start.
Clearer roles
Define them at the start, so ownership is settled before the build begins.
A status template
Use a daily written one, so every handoff note has the same shape.
